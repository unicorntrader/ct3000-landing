What are Callouts?

4 min read

Most trading apps will show you a chart of your equity curve and call it a day. You already know if you're up or down — that's not the hard part. The hard part is knowing why, and whether the reason is something you can actually fix.

Callouts are CoTrader's answer to that. They're twelve deterministic rules that run against your own trade history — not a market feed, not generic advice, your actual fills — and surface the patterns that are real enough to matter.

Deterministic means no guessing

"Deterministic" is the important word here. Each rule is a fixed condition checked against your data: a symbol with an unusually high win rate across enough trades to trust it, a weekday that's quietly dragging your average down, a cluster of entries opened within an hour of a loss. If the condition isn't met, the callout doesn't fire. There's no AI guessing at sentiment, no vague "you might want to consider" — either the pattern shows up in your numbers or it doesn't.

That's a deliberate choice. Generic trading advice is easy to ignore because it isn't about you. A callout that says "AAPL: 75% win rate across 4 trades" is impossible to ignore, because it's your own account making the point.

What the twelve rules actually cover

The rules span three areas:

  • Symbols — which names you're actually good at trading, and which ones you keep returning to without an edge.
  • Timing — days of the week, times of day, or streaks that correlate with worse outcomes.
  • Behavior — patterns like revenge trading, oversized positions after a loss, or entries with no plan attached.

Each one comes with a "why flagged" explanation and a concrete "what to try" suggestion — not a lecture, just the next experiment worth running on your own process.

Why this matters more than a P&L chart

A P&L chart tells you the score. Callouts tell you the game. Most traders who improve don't do it by finding a new strategy — they do it by removing two or three specific habits that were quietly costing them money. You can't remove what you can't see, and most of it doesn't show up until someone — or something — actually looks at the pattern across dozens of trades instead of one at a time.

That's the whole idea behind CoTrader's Insights row: twelve rules, running silently on your own history, surfacing the handful of things that are actually worth acting on.
